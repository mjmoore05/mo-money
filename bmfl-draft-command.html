What this is

A single tool for running your auction: it records every pick and, from that same log, tells you what each player is worth to you and whether to keep bidding. Nothing needs the internet and nothing is stored anywhere but this browser.

The one rule: the pick log is the only thing that matters. Every budget, roster and recommendation on screen is recalculated from it, so nothing can drift out of step with what actually happened. Protect the pick log and everything else takes care of itself.

Quick start

  1. Check the setup. Gear icon → Setup. Confirm the ten owner names, the $200 budget and the $1 minimum bid. Do this before pick one — renaming an owner mid-draft orphans their picks.
  2. Player list is already loaded. 215 players with values built for this league. You only need Gear → Player sheet if you want to load your own CSV instead.
  3. Record a pick. Click the player in the pool (or type any name), tap the winning owner, type the price, press Enter.
  4. Watch the save chip. Top right. Green "Saved" after every pick. If it ever goes red, stop and export before entering anything else.
  5. Export every ten picks. The tool nags you. Let it.

Reading the advisor

Everything is labelled arithmetic or model. Arithmetic follows from the picks and involves no judgement. Model rests on assumptions that are stated next to the number. Argue with the model panels; trust the arithmetic ones.

Bid check — the panel you use most

Click a player, then type what the bidding is currently at. You get a call:

BID $22
Go one more. Shows how much headroom is left and what winning leaves you per remaining slot.
Last call
The next bid is at your ceiling. Win it here or let it go — do not chase past this.
PASS
Past your number, with how far past and who is likely to take him instead.

Above the verdict sits your bid-to number and the multiple of market it represents, with every reason the premium moved off 1.00× listed underneath — positional squeeze, a tier cliff, what the room has actually been paying.

The three ceilings

Balanced
Pay this and every remaining slot still fills at market. No concentration.
Your plan
Built from your budget buckets — finishes the anchors and mid starters you still owe yourself, then goes cheap.
Hard cap
Pure arithmetic: your money minus $1 for each remaining slot. Not advice.

The headline number is the smaller of what he is worth and what you can afford, and the panel says which one is binding — including your top-star ceiling of $55.

Budget plan

Your buckets, tracked live. Picks fall into buckets by price, so the panel shows the shape of your roster rather than what you meant to do. The projected leftover row is the one to watch: over $5 and you are set to walk out with money you could have spent.

Market

Inflation compares money still unspent against value still on the board. When the room overpays early, everything left gets cheaper and the tool reprices it. Last 12 picks shows what the room actually paid against par — when the two disagree, believe this one.

Nominate these

Ranked by drain: market price times the number of rivals who still need that position and can afford him. Top rows are positions you do not need — throw those out. Rows flagged red are ones you do need; stay quiet about those.

Standings

Projected points per week from each owner's current starting nine, with empty slots valued at replacement so a half-built roster is compared fairly. This is the only panel that answers "am I winning".

Recording picks

A player who is not in the list

Type the name in the bid panel, pick the position from the dropdown, record as normal. The player list is a convenience, never a requirement — nothing breaks.

Fixing a mistake

Every row in the pick log has an ×. Click it, then click remove to confirm. You can remove any pick, not just the last one; the remaining picks renumber and every total recalculates. A typo on pick 12 does not mean unwinding thirty picks.

What the pool columns mean

PTS/GM
Projected fantasy points per game in this league's scoring — half PPR, 4-point passing touchdowns. A dash means the player came from the ADP list with no projection behind him.
EST $
Auction value computed for 10 teams, $200, half PPR, no kicker. Not what he will go for — what he is worth.

Sharing and syncing

Use the Share button in the app bar. It copies the entire draft as a single line of text. Anyone paste it into their copy of either tool and they are exactly current.

The protocol that avoids arguments:

  • One person enters every pick. Everyone else is a mirror.
  • That person posts a sync code to the league chat every ten picks or so.
  • If their laptop dies, the last posted code becomes the new master.

Loading a sync code replaces the draft in that copy — it does not merge. That is deliberate: merging two versions of the truth is exactly the ambiguity worth avoiding. Check the pick count in the confirmation message; if it is lower than you expected, you loaded an old code.

If something goes wrong

Save chip turns red. Export JSON immediately, before entering another pick. Your picks are still on screen — they just are not written to disk.

Browser closes or crashes. Reopen the file. Everything up to the last saved pick is there. Five rolling snapshots cover roughly the last twenty-five picks.

Moving the file after the draft orphans the saved picks. The browser keys storage to the file's location. Your exports travel; the saved state does not. Pick a folder before draft day and leave it there.

Keep a paper log at the table. No browser tool is proof against a spilled drink, and reconciling against a written list takes seconds.

Settings in the app bar

Owner
Which seat the advice is written from. Set this to yourself.
Stars / Mids
Your roster shape. These reshape the budget buckets directly — slots always sum to 15. Defaults of 2 and 4 reproduce the plan: 2 anchors $100–115, 1 second-tier $30–35, 4 mids $40–50, QB $1–10, DEF $1, 6 bench $6–12.
Heat
How hard your league bids relative to fair value. 110 is set for a room that pays over the odds at the top. The tool also measures this from the last twelve picks on its own.

Where the numbers came from

215 players. Projections are half PPR and were verified against this league's exact scoring to within 0.4 points across a full season. Auction values use value-based drafting with replacement levels set by this league — 10 teams, no kicker — which is what a generic 12-team sheet gets wrong. ADP comes from 3,140 ten-team half-PPR mock drafts.

The Source column in the exported CSV tells you what is behind each row: exact verified stat lines, fitted calibrated to about half a point per game, noconv uncalibrated QB and DEF, ranked and adp real players with a rank-derived price and no projection.

Caps and settings were tuned by simulating drafts against a room that bids aggressively at the top. The rival behaviour in those simulations is a model, not your league. Treat the settings as a good starting point, not gospel — and override any number when you know something the tool does not.

Saved automatically after every pick, with five rolling snapshots. That is not a substitute for exporting. Export at least every 10 picks and keep a paper log at the table.
